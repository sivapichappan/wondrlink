Sage has a character, or it has none

Three directions with an actual point of view. Not three temperatures of the same idea.

What I heard. The first set was safe: one was the current app with better manners, one was a dark mode, and large print was a legibility spec wearing a costume. Large print is gone and is not coming back. Each of these commits to something instead, and each says plainly what it costs.

What none of them touch. Sixth-grade copy, the frozen crisis machinery, and the rule that the hardest moments get no flourish at all. Flavour lives in the type, the ground and the accent. It never lives in an animation next to a prognosis wall.

One

Kept — the notebook you carry

Everybody in treatment ends up with a folder. Appointment cards, a printout with a phone number circled, questions scribbled the night before. Sage is trying to replace that folder, so it should look like the thing it replaces — ruled paper, a tab-coloured spine on anything to act on, your own words underlined in ink like you wrote them there. The green goes: this is ink blue on cream, and it reads as something personal rather than as software with a brand.

Two

The Reader — the answer is the headline

The most direct attack on the thing the test group actually complained about. If the product's job is to make one clear sentence out of a wall of clinical language, then that sentence should be set like a headline and everything else should get out of its way. Big editorial serif for the answer, a proper reading face beneath it, hairline rules, one oxblood accent doing all the work. Nearly monochrome, and confident about it. Nothing here looks like a chat app, which is the point.

Three

Lamplight — warm, and awake at 2am

The dark direction from the first set, but with an actual atmosphere instead of a dark-grey palette. Nothing on this screen is blue. The ground is a warm brown-black with a low bloom, as if a lamp is on beside the bed, and the single amber light in the room is where your own words are. Sage speaks in warm cream serif. It is designed for the hour when someone is awake, frightened, and does not want to be handed a lit torch.
