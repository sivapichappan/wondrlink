Three directions for Sage

The same two moments rendered three ways: the conversation with a card dealt into it, and the hardest question the product ever gets asked. Each direction is grounded in a claim about who is holding the phone, not in a mood.

What is already true regardless of which you pick. The motion work has shipped into the branch: every surface now answers to touch, the cards actually deal rather than blink into existence, the check-in crossfades between questions, and the launch no longer flashes white. None of that changes with the palette, which is why it was built first.

Direction A

Paper, finished

The approved direction, taken to the end it was already pointing at. Same paper ground, same sage, same rule that the warm bubble is only ever your words. What changes is the craft the build dropped: the negative tracking the mockups specified, a real leading scale, and body type up from 15 to 17.

  • Body 17pt
  • Source Serif 4 · Instrument Sans
  • Paper #F6F7F3
  • Light only

The cost: it is not really a new direction. If the current app feels flat to you, this will feel like the current app with better manners.

Direction B

Night ward

Grounded in a fact about this product rather than a taste: the app is pinned to light mode and a great many of these conversations happen at two in the morning. Someone awake in bed, frightened, holding a phone at full brightness, is a real and frequent user and the current design has nothing for them. Dark first, warm rather than blue-black, and the patient's own words are the one warm light on the screen.

  • Body 17pt
  • Dark first, light follows
  • Ground #121714
  • Warm bubble glows

The cost: two palettes to maintain, every screen checked twice, and the contrast work has to be redone from scratch. Roughly double the visual QA.

Direction C

Large print

The most literal answer to the brief's own thesis, that the app demanded too much of a stressed patient. Body type at 21pt, chips big enough for an unsteady hand, ink on near-white at maximum contrast, and Sage's messages set as plain text rather than in bubbles, because the conversation is the page. Chemotherapy affects concentration and vision; this direction treats that as the design constraint rather than an accessibility setting.

  • Body 21pt
  • Newsreader · Instrument Sans
  • Near-black on #FBFAF7
  • Fewer elements per screen

The cost: much less fits on a screen, so more scrolling and harder layout decisions everywhere. It will read as an app for old people to some, which is a real product risk worth saying out loud.

Rendered for a decision, not for production.
